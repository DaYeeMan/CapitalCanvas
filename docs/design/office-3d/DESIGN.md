# Office architecture and behavior

The current implementation is the React/Three office homepage at `/`, including the city-backed About page at `#about`. The retired `/preview/office` route replaces its history entry with `/`, preserving query/hash. Tool routes remain independent. The maintained After Hours references live in [the reference gallery](../office-redesign/REFERENCES.md); current behavior and layout are defined by code and the notes below.

## Scene and layout

The preview uses the original white/cyan CC image, locally hosted Geist, always-visible top-right Home / Resources / About navigation, and bottom-left Back to city on the floors. About is a functional page over the same closed city.

`TOWER_SITE` fixes the tower at x/z = −1.5m/−1.5m, away from the city camera. `TOWER_PLOT` trims 1.7m from the rear paving, preserving the front landscaping while leaving a full 1.2m rear sidewalk and 1.5m to the front asphalt. `cityPlot.ts` fills the surrounding sidewalk at the existing paving height, with a curved hole matching the rounded curb exactly. The sidewalk reaches the left neighbour's paved edge and fills both curved corner cutouts along that connection. Street strips exclude this shared region, preventing gaps or overlapping surfaces at the corners. The exterior and both office slices share one parent transform; entrance, planting and the four plot lights follow the same site position. Roads and neighbouring buildings stay fixed. Office/inspection camera bounds, directional lighting and tower haze masks follow the setback, while landing framing remains anchored to the district. Relative floor heights and journey timing are unchanged.

Both floors share a 20x20 metre square shell, 0.32m walls, 0.58m front cutaway walls, and 2.9225m tall caps. `layouts.ts` owns dimensions, fixtures, rooms, and seating anchors. `OfficeShell.tsx`, `OfficeFloor.tsx`, and `Furniture.tsx` render those descriptors. `officeSpatial.ts` defines the corresponding solid footprints, seat positions, doorway crossings, and approach/departure points; rugs and room interiors are traversable.

The visual system uses cream tile and walls, pale wood, navy upholstery, blue screens, faceted plants, and single-plane glass. Interior and exterior glass use separate materials. Cream exterior frames surround broad windows, with floor-specific opening spans. Furniture geometry, materials, textures, charts, and foliage are cached in `sceneResources.ts`.

Office close-ups use the After Hours graphite/blue-grey atmosphere and original orthographic framing, with the lower tower facade continuing below the viewport. Every visible task lamp supplies local ivory lighting. Lounge/reading floor lamps and Home's central seating lamp create warmer pools against the cool neutral floor; their bases share navigation footprints. Selected warm windows and street fixtures light the district. The city follows the Grounded District reference: four detailed neighbours with vertical, framed and curtain-wall window rhythms; recessed blue glazing with dark mullions and pale reflections; cream piers, sills and spandrels on neighbouring buildings sharing the office exterior wall material; parapets and ventilated roof equipment; entrance canopies; rounded paved sidewalks, crossings and lane markings; planted ribbons, shrubs and clustered faceted trees. Eighteen blank slate-blue context volumes (#596b7c, lightened to follow REFERENCES.md) without windows establish streets beyond the detailed district without adding occupied floors. Repeated parts are instanced by geometry/material, with two additional shared sidewalk/foliage geometries. `towerStructure.ts` preserves all tower dimensions and places Resources 23.8 metres below Home with six closed intervening storeys; `TowerAssembly.tsx` owns the persistent exterior assemblies. `cameraFit.ts` supplies the existing city/inspection and office fits while the motion driver and settled office controls share camera ownership. The city camera uses a 10% smaller viewing height with a slight upward/leftward reframe; office and whole-tower viewing scale and angle remain unchanged, with their framing centered on the site. `CityAtmosphere.ts` renders every view through continuous perimeter/aerial haze and a subtle background blur that rejects foreground tower samples, followed by colour-correct output. The shared window texture adds an uninterrupted graduated ivory glow and darker edge reveals, with no brown lower-corner silhouettes. A project-authored dusk environment provides rough reflections, depth-based contact shading grounds nearby surfaces, and a high-threshold bloom pass gives bright fixtures/windows a restrained halo. All thirteen streetlights illuminate nearby surfaces. The lighting palette and exposure remain consistent across city, Home, Resources and inspection. Warm-neutral lamp falloff and quieter stone-tile variation reduce desk hotspots and floor contrast. The district remains present throughout city/floor travel; atmosphere fades the surroundings without a visibility cutoff. City and both floor-light shader configurations are preloaded into the same linear render-target pipeline before the first journey.

Office point-light contribution is isolated from exterior materials in `officeLightIsolation.ts`. The existing street/office height separation identifies point-light families in world coordinates; moving the tower or camera does not change eligibility. New point-light families must preserve or revise this explicit height-band contract. `officeLighting.ts` fades task/lounge intensity by facade-to-slice clearance (zero through 5cm, full at 1.2m), restoring cached authored strengths on reopening. Moving exterior pieces retain their authored shadow flags throughout travel. The key light stays beyond the raised assemblies with continuous shadow coverage, avoiding the old binary shadow switch. Geometry, settled lighting palette and journey timing remain unchanged.

`officeWallHandoff.ts` uses the same `FACADE_BAND` dimensions as the exterior to conceal the two front cutaway walls before the interior is hidden. Their floor-anchored height eases from 0.58m to 0.08m as clearance closes from 1.2m to 5cm; a matching 1cm inward offset avoids a coplanar seam. At the visibility cutoff they fit behind the opaque band. The physical-gap calculation reverses without separate timing, and fully open geometry retains its original bounds. Collision layouts and fallback stills stay unchanged.

Landing colour grading in `CityAtmosphere.ts` adds a dark navy copy side and a broad blue-grey field centered around the city. Background and distance haze share that field. Local midtone lifting/desaturation improves city surfaces while limiting the adjustment on deep glazing and bright emission. A separate `landing` uniform follows the journey's actual city presence; it is zero on both floors and inspection, independently of the inspection haze blend. Entry and return therefore retain continuous atmosphere without altering office lighting or exposure.

## Mobile framing and layout

The responsive fit in `cameraFit.ts` brings portrait city views 35–50% closer and office base fits 25% closer, with smaller 12%/10% increases for short landscape. City zoom varies continuously with available phone height. Phones start each office floor at 2×, cropping the raised tower while retaining all three desk markers. `officeZoomRange` owns the default/minimum/maximum: phone 2×/1×/4×, desktop and tablet 1×/1×/2×. `fittedCamera` returns the actual arrival pose; controls recover its base height by multiplying by the default zoom, avoiding a doubled scale or an arrival snap. Desktop geometry, camera angle and framing are unchanged.

Portrait desk previews dock below the desks; short landscape previews sit at the right edge with a narrower width where needed. Both have a close control, outside-touch dismissal, and no mouse hover connector. Mobile header shading preserves text contrast over the enlarged tower. About uses an internally scrollable reading area below the header and above its footer; footer links wrap inside the viewport. Floor canvases capture touch input for bounded one-finger pan and two-finger zoom anchored to the moving midpoint. Pinch-to-pan continuation preserves the remaining finger's position; cancellation, navigation, resize and visibility changes release captures. Native scrolling remains available in research libraries and About.

## Navigation and interaction

The shell no longer owns inspection state or advertises it in keyboard instructions. Existing internal inspection camera helpers remain unexposed; normal floor framing, navigation, desk interactions and city journeys continue unchanged.

`officePage.ts` distinguishes the selected page from the physical city/Home/Resources view. About maps to the city; landing/About swaps retain the same scene command, camera and parallax state. A reversible 280ms clock updates the HTML blend and wakes demand rendering through a scoped event, without per-frame React updates. `OfficeSite.tsx` overlays the selected editorial note, existing contact details and compact legal footer using the same Geist family/weights/palette. Hidden outgoing content is inert and excluded from accessibility. About has no inspection or extra return control; the logo returns to landing, and the tower/Home/Resources provide floor entry.

`CityAtmosphere.ts` extends the reading shade through 46% of the viewport and feathers it to zero by 62%, using the existing navy colour. Camera, physical lights, materials, bloom and right-hand city remain unchanged. During a floor return the About shade, note and footer follow the existing final-pullback copy progress. Floor entry removes the additional shade; settled floors/inspection retain zero About contribution. Static About uses the existing city JPEG with an HTML/CSS darkening layer and live content/navigation. See the [About implementation plan and checks](../office-redesign/about/README.md).

`OfficeSite.tsx` owns the native header, accessible stage, lazy canvas boundary, and WebGL fallback. `useOfficeNavigation.ts` owns loading/moving/idle destinations, latest intent, history and skip/preference handling. `towerMotion.ts` defines deterministic staged journeys; `TowerMotionDriver.tsx` applies persistent assembly/office transforms and owns the camera until the actual destination frame is presented. Camera fits are shared in `cameraFit.ts`; office controls resume after arrival. The same controls own a 720ms, reversible inspection camera transition from the actual pan/zoom; logarithmic scale interpolation keeps the visual zoom steady. Escape returns to the office, and new floor selections can interrupt inspection. Reduced motion or a hidden page settles inspection immediately. Desk interactions stay hidden during the return. Inspection blends shadow coverage and atmospheric softness continuously with camera framing. Stale acknowledgements are ignored. See [motion structure and verification](MOTION.md).

`OfficeViewControls.tsx` and `officeView.ts` provide cursor-anchored wheel zoom, midpoint-anchored pinch, bounded pointer drag, elastic overshoot, keyboard controls, and reset to the viewport's default on floor selection or keyboard 0. Phone resize retains user zoom; switching between phone and desktop ranges restores the new default. The isometric angle stays fixed. Pins remain constant in CSS-pixel size while their world anchors and desk hit regions follow projection. `LocationMarker.tsx` supports whole-desk/pin hover, keyboard focus, and Escape dismissal. A compact actionable panel opens the tool on Home and its existing research anchor on Resources. Hover and keyboard focus share one active panel. Panels prefer the outer side of each anchor, use their measured size for header/footer clearance, and provide a hoverable connector onto the action. Closing panels become inert immediately, support Escape and remain within the viewport. Camera manipulation clears stale keyboard intent rather than reopening a dismissed panel. Transitions and camera manipulation temporarily suppress disclosure.

Each initial destination opening uses one shared easing progress for its upper assembly/panels (+4.4 m) and slice (+2.2 m), making compact equal gaps. The camera holds for that reveal, then zooms while the slice stays put and the upper assembly continues to +32 m. Floor-to-floor closure brings the slice and upper assembly down together with slight camera pullback before the facade traverse. Each settled-floor city return reverses entry's structural order in two stages: a 1.55s pullback holds the slice up while the upper assembly returns to the compact split, then a 1.05s final pullback closes all gaps together. The landing headline, explanation and entry button reveal from the same sampled progress as that final camera movement; there is no separate CSS settle. Early reversed entries close directly when already in compact city framing. Resize preserves copy progress and skip/reduced motion settle it immediately. Static fallback reports availability to the shell, uses current-scene stills and omits 3D inspection/camera instructions; dormant HTML inside a working canvas does not report a failure.

## People, seating, and doors

Each floor has nine permanent identities and an independent random seed. Refresh creates new assignments; no identity, assignment, or behavior history is persisted. Every person has a unique home desk but can borrow any available seat. Roughly one third of eligible sitting trips prefer home. All desks, conference chairs, armchairs, sofa places, and coffee stools participate; rooms use the same random rules.

`peoplePaths.ts` builds a cached sampled navigation graph with exact seat and doorway anchors. A* routes are checked against actual obstacles and animated body/stride envelopes. People wait, yield, or reroute around occupied paths. `peopleSimulation.ts` runs fixed 100ms decisions and collision checks, reserves seats on approach, and releases them on departure or cancellation. People sit for 10-20 seconds, walk at 0.95m/s, and occasionally stand for 1-3 seconds. Sitting/walking balance is a soft preference, not a quota.

Doors pause approaching walkers while opening, admit followers traveling in the same direction, yield opposing traffic, and close after clearance. Only the leaf and handle rotate; frames and transoms stay fixed. Collision checks include the moving leaf.

`OfficePeople.tsx` renders cloned rigs with shared geometry and clips. Animations sample continuous session time each display frame; position, turns, and stride blending interpolate between simulation steps. Walk playback matches the measured 1.96m cycle distance of each rig. Periodic keyboard typing is a small forearm oscillation. Non-keyboard seats use resting poses, and sit/stand transitions switch directly between poses.

Sofa poses use level thighs, vertical calves, and raised feet. Hip height is 5cm above the preceding floor-reaching pose, allowing the models to sit about 30cm farther back. Rig-specific anchors retain a measured buffer from the sofa base. The collision envelope includes the raised feet. Person and sofa dimensions remain unchanged.

## Lifecycle and resource ownership

`PeopleSession` owns both logical floor simulations. Hidden pages perform cheap periodic behavior work without WebGL or mixers. Returning frames catch up elapsed time in bounded batches before presentation. Only the visible floor applies mixers. Reduced motion pauses session time and freezes poses and doors.

The canvas renders continuously for visible office motion and on demand when paused or in the city. Camera projection avoids unnecessary DOM updates and includes the selected floor's tower elevation. Shared scene resources persist across floor switches; per-person mixers, skeleton resources, and facade instance buffers are disposed on unmount. Scene postprocessing is created during shader warmup, resizes with the viewport/DPR, and blends the district haze and background softness continuously with camera travel. It disposes its passes, the bloom high-pass material and render targets on unmount. History/visibility listeners and input handlers clean up. `OfficeFallback.tsx` uses local screenshots of the current district and floors and shares preview navigation when WebGL fails.
